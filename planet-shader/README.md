# planet-shader

A whole planet in one WebGL2 fragment shader, no mesh and no texture. Every pixel shoots a ray at a ball; where it hits, the surface point (a unit vector) is rotated into the planet's own frame and fed to 3D gradient noise (hash, quintic fade, 12 gradient directions, fractal sum of up to 8 octaves). The height gives colour bands (sea, beach, lowland, rock, snow, polar ice), a bump normal from two extra lookups gives relief, a second noise layer gives clouds with shadows, the sun gives a soft day/night terminator with city lights and a glow shell fakes the atmosphere. The number of octaves follows the pixel size, so the detail fades in when you zoom. The mapping chip switches to the naive way, longitude and latitude as flat noise coordinates, where the seam and the pinched poles show up (marked in orange). Drag orbits the camera, the wheel zooms, the chips pick the planet (Earth, Desert, Ice, Lava), mapping, octaves, atmosphere, clouds, spin and render resolution. The math is mirrored on the CPU and tested natively (`test.cpp`: hash and noise values, noise against a plain reference, fbm sums and the detail fade, the seam jump, ray and camera geometry, projection round trip, the planet frame, angle reduction, glow and colour bands), the rendering needs a browser.
