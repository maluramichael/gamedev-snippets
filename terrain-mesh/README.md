# terrain-mesh

A height field cut into chunks, each chunk a grid mesh (WebGL2 / OpenGL ES 3). The world is 512 x 512 m, 256 x 256 cells of 2 m, chunks of 16, 32 or 64 cells, every chunk built at three levels of detail (every vertex, every 2nd, every 4th) into one vertex buffer; the camera picks a level per chunk by its distance to the chunk's nearest point. The height is seven octaves of value noise. The chips switch the two classic mistakes: "local" gives every chunk noise coordinates that start at its own origin (every chunk is the same hill, with a step at every edge), "skirts off" shows the cracks where a coarse chunk meets a fine one. Skirts are walls that hang 7 m below the chunk edges, orange in the wireframe. Normals come from the height function one finest cell to each side, even beyond the chunk, so neighbours of any level get the same vector. The camera flies a circle until you drag: drag to look, WASD or the arrows to fly, E and Q up and down, wheel forward; keys C coords, K skirts, V view, space flying/free, 1 2 3 chunk size. The mesh maths is tested natively (`test.cpp`: noise by hand, shared edge vertices and normals across chunks and levels, the crack width along all edges of the world against the skirt depth, winding and edge counts of the meshes, LOD choice, camera), the rendering needs a browser.
