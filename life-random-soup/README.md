# life-random-soup

48 random soups of the Game of Life (16x16 cells, every cell alive with the chosen density) run side by side on an unbounded plane, so gliders can fly away without hitting a wall. A tile turns teal when its population has repeated with a period of at most 30 for 120 generations in a row (ash and gliders left over), grey when it died out, and its lifespan goes into the histogram; then a fresh soup replaces it. In 2,000 soups at 50 percent only 4 died out completely, the median lifespan is 254 generations and 13 percent live past 1,103, the lifespan of the R-pentomino, which runs next to them (or the acorn: 5,206 generations). The chips pick the density, the comparison pattern and the speed in generations per second. The histogram starts too short because long soups are still running, give it a few seconds. The world, the settle detector and the statistics are in `logic.hpp` and covered by `test.cpp`, whose reference numbers (R-pentomino 1,103 and 116 cells, acorn 5,206 and 633, 50 soups) come from a separate Python implementation. `test --stats` prints the lifespan tables behind the numbers above.
