# integration-methods

One planet on a circular orbit and one mass on a spring, each simulated by four integrators at once from the same start: explicit Euler, semi-implicit Euler (the same two lines in the other order), Verlet and RK4. Both systems conserve their energy exactly in real life, so the relative energy drift shown as a number and as a graph (signed log scale, one decade per quarter of the height) is the error of the method. Explicit Euler adds energy every step, the planet spirals outwards and the spring swings wider. Semi-implicit Euler and Verlet keep the energy bounded for good, RK4 is the most exact but slowly drains energy at large steps. The dt chips (0.02 to 0.4) change the time step and restart the run, because Verlet cannot change its step in the middle. Keys: Space pause, R restart, left/right dt, 1 to 4 show or hide a method. The simulation, the guards (flown-away bodies are frozen, a pass through the sun does not divide by zero) and the number formatting are in `logic.hpp` and covered by `test.cpp`, with hand-computed single steps, the exact energy factor of explicit Euler on the spring, the bound of semi-implicit Euler and the order of accuracy of each method.
