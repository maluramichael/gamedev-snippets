# ray-marching

Sphere tracing through signed distance fields, all in one WebGL2 fragment shader: a floor plus a sphere, a rounded box and a torus that merge with a polynomial smooth union while they move. Every pixel shoots a ray and repeats `t += sdf(p)` until the distance drops below a threshold, the ray leaves the scene or the step budget runs out. Normals come from the gradient of the field (tetrahedron taps), soft shadows and ambient occlusion from a few extra lookups along a ray. Drag orbits the camera, the wheel zooms, the chips switch a step-count heatmap, shadows, the blend strength, the step limit and the render resolution. The shader math is mirrored on the CPU and tested natively (`test.cpp`: exact distances for sphere, box and torus, `smin` bounds, step counts of `march`, normals, shadows, AO, and a Lipschitz check of the whole scene), the rendering needs a browser. Post: [Ray marching](https://malura.de/en/blog/ray-marching).
